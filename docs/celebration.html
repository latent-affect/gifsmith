GIFsmith turns a video into a GIF where the captions land exactly when people actually speak. Feed it an .srt or .vtt, or let local whisper.cpp and sherpa-onnx transcribe and diarize your clip on-device, speaker labels and all, as a timing scaffold you edit into your own lines. Each caption fires and cuts precisely on cue, not eyeballed. Stitch up to eight clips into one GIF, pick classic meme style or a caption bar, preview the exact pixels before you commit, and get real per-platform autoplay-size verdicts for X, Discord, Tumblr, GIPHY, and Mastodon instead of guessing whether your GIF will even play. The whole thing runs on your machine: a Go server bound to 127.0.0.1 with zero network egress, so nothing you upload to it ever leaves your computer, unlike basically every web-based GIF or caption tool. It's free, open source, has zero third-party Go dependencies and zero JS dependencies, and produces byte-identical output for the same input every time. If you've ever hand-timed captions to dialogue in a video editor, this does that part for you. Locally. Privately. For free.
